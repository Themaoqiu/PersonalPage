<PaperCard
  title={"Interactive Visual Gym: Evaluating Visual Interaction Models with Simulated Users in Interactive Worlds"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08286"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.08286"}
  summary={"Visual interaction models that support users during ongoing physical tasks require evaluation in closed loop, where assistant responses can change subsequent user behavior and task outcomes. Existing benchmarks largely rely on offline replay and therefore cannot capture these effects. We introduce Interactive Visual Gym, a framework for closed-loop evaluation and training of visual interaction models through joint simulation of executable task environments and human users. We contribute three simulators that recreate everyday tasks and human-aligned action spaces across cooking, household manipulation, and mobile-interface domains, together with a persona-conditioned user simulation harness that collaborates with the assistant while incorporating controlled execution errors and other out-of-plan behaviors. We instantiate the framework as IVG-Bench, a benchmark with 315 interaction scenarios. Evaluating state-of-the-art VLMs as visual interaction models reveals substantial limitations: the best-performing model, Gemini 4 Argon, completes only 53.4% of tasks within the time limit, exhibits significant weaknesses in factual grounding and actionable guidance, and is often too slow to prevent errors when response latency is considered. We verify our evaluation protocol with a human study which finds 78.5% agreement between our interaction-quality metric and participant preferences. We further show that the environments provide useful learning signals: in a proof-of-concept GRPO experiment, closed-loop training improves both task success and interaction quality for Qwen3.5-4B model in our cooking environment."}

<PaperCard
  title={"Where Does Alignment Emerge and What Does It Mean? A Closer Look at the Platonic Representation Hypothesis"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12869"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.12869"}
  summary={"The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that neural networks are converging toward similar representation spaces. As language models become more capable, their representations are aligning with those of vision models. However, recent work has questioned whether this trend continues to hold for newer language models. Moreover, it has been shown that representation similarity metrics can be confounded by model depth and width, requiring appropriate baselines for interpretation. In this work, we first investigate where alignment emerges between unimodal models across model layers and representation dimensions. Next, we find that the PRH capability-alignment trend persists for newer models as long as the captions are sufficiently clean and informative. Finally, we contextualize cross-modal alignment, introducing unimodal and multimodal baselines on various datasets. Our results show that the cross-modal representational alignment of unimodal models can surpass that of explicitly aligned vision-language models, but remains substantially behind the alignment observed for models of the same modality. Code is available at https://github.com/ExplainableML/prh-closer-look."}

<PaperCard
  title={"Subliminal Learning in Vision-Language Models"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08538"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.08538"}
  summary={"Subliminal learning is a phenomenon in large language models (LLMs) where a teacher passes a hidden preference to a student through training data unrelated to that preference. We study whether subliminal learning also occurs in vision-language models (VLMs). We finetune a teacher VLM to prefer a specific animal or facial attribute and use a control teacher from the same base model. Both teachers generate numbers for an unrelated visual task involving geometric shapes. Two students initialized from the same base VLM are finetuned on the same images, using numbers generated by either the biased or control teacher. Across VLMs ranging from 2B to 7B parameters, we find that both animal and facial preferences can transfer through this unrelated task. Students trained on biased-teacher outputs select the target more often than control students, with a difference of up to $\\sim$10 percentage points in the animal preference task. Transfer can become stronger with more training data and varies with the target preference, model, and how the numbers are generated. Beyond preferences, we show that the biased student's explanations use more of the same words and phrases as the biased teacher's explanations than those of the control student, even when all three models select the same image. These results matter for training VLMs and show that training on unrelated data may change both a VLM's image preferences and how it explains its choices, and that transfer can become stronger with more training samples."}

<PaperCard
  title={"SteerDistill: Quantizing LLM Agents via Closed-Loop Teacher Steering"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13410"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.13410"}
  summary={"Low-bit quantization reduces the inference cost of LLM agents but can compromise task success. Examining failed rollouts, we find that quantized agents overlook task requirements or environmental conditions, producing environment transitions that diverge from successful full-precision references. Correcting these behavioral divergences remains difficult: offline data may miss the student's failure contexts, while its own rollouts may provide few correct responses in those contexts. We introduce SteerDistill, a quantization-aware distillation method in which a full-precision teacher guides the student to revise failed responses and iteratively refines its guidance using feedback from student execution. This closed-loop teacher steering produces locally verified behavioral corrections. We internalize these corrections by training the student without guidance to match the steered student's predictions at corrected turns and the full-precision teacher's at other turns, enabling unassisted inference. Across five agentic benchmarks and three models, SteerDistill exceeds the strongest quantized baseline by 1.67--2.18 points in average score under W4A16 and stays within one point of BF16. On $τ^2$-Bench, it outperforms QeRL by 1.84 percentage points in success rate using 67% fewer training GPU hours and produces shorter successful trajectories than the quantized baselines."}

  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22237"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2608.22237"}
  summary={"Web-facing AI agents repeatedly access webpages, documents, repositories, and tool responses, yet reading interfaces often expose entire objects when only sparse evidence is needed. This over-reading increases token and latency costs and can dilute relevant evidence. We present SparseRead, a reusable reading control layer that governs content admission without modifying the agent model. Its Read Intent Protocol tracks evolving needs and source coverage; Adaptive Reader Backends select how evidence is located and exposed; and the Budgeted Read Gate bounds cumulative raw exposure per episode with faithful full-view fallback. A unified Reader interface supports interchangeable evidence acquisition across heterogeneous resources. Across six models, including Claude Opus 5, and five scenarios, SparseRead reduces total tokens by up to 92.9% and wall time by up to 89.0%, preserving or improving task quality in 97% settings. On Web deep-search tasks, it reduces tokens by 31.0--43.0% and task completion time by 23.0--37.0%. Ablations validate the three designs, while integration with three agent frameworks demonstrates portability."}

<PaperCard
  title={"Evolving Infra: Agent-Adaptive LLM Serving at Scale"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12304"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.12304"}
  summary={"Agentic workloads are a major source of LLM inference demand. Existing prefill--decode (PD) disaggregated serving systems offer limited flexibility for runtime adaptation of compute allocation and KV cache management, and rely on coarse signals that only partly capture the diverse and dynamic agent workload. These limitations lead to load imbalance between prefill and decode nodes and eviction of KV caches that agents will reuse. We present Evolving Infra, an agent-adaptive serving framework that adapts compute allocation to agent workloads and KV cache management to agent context management at runtime. We decouple compute allocation from key--value (KV) cache management, allowing nodes to switch efficiently between prefill and decode. Building on this design, an online planner matches the PD split to workload composition, while an agent-aware distributed KV manager guides cache eviction and transfer with signals from agent context management. Across coding, math, and computer-use tasks with GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 on up to 256 GB300 GPUs, Evolving Infra reduces mean end-to-end request latency and P95 TTFT by up to 4.1x and 14x over Dynamo, NVIDIA's production framework."}

  summary={"Large language models (LLMs) draw on parametric knowledge and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to solve knowledge-intensive tasks.   summary={"Large language models (LLMs) increasingly support Web-based services that must balance response quality and inference cost across heterogeneous requests.
