<PaperCard
  title={"Probing the \"Conscious\" Mind: Selective Access and Global Workspace in Vision-Language Models"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26590"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.26590"}
  summary={"Vision-language models (VLMs) integrate visual and textual information, but their outputs do not reveal internal concept representations. Global workspace theory (GWT) proposes that information is selected from parallel brain processes, integrated in a shared workspace, and made available to specialised processes. We introduce the Multimodal Concept Lens (MC-Lens), a multimodal Jacobian lens, to study these functions in VLM concept processing. Its vision encoder and language decoder lenses map intermediate states to a shared semantic space defined by the decoder's output vocabulary to compare concept accessibility across modalities. MC-Lens supports internal concept interventions to examine the role of accessed concepts in downstream computation. (i) Accessibility and selective access. Multimodal concept information is readable in intermediate states and selectively accessible as tasks require. We extend \"readout carriers\" from one name token to a token set to better capture and express concept information and support counterfactual experiments. (ii) Concepts in downstream computation. Counterfactual experiments show that accessed concept information participates in downstream computation and reasoning. (iii) Development of cross-modal alignment and selective access. We develop two J-space metrics tracking the development of cross-modal concept alignment more clearly than hidden state cosine in controlled VLM training. The experiment also shows that reliable selective access develops later than concept accessibility. (iv) Shared functions across models. Across VLM families and sizes, we find shared patterns of concept accessibility, query-dependent selectivity and cross-task intervention effects despite differences in internal organisation. These findings offer an experimental basis to study GWT in multimodal artificial systems and how it can guide cross-modal alignment improvements."}

<PaperCard
  title={"The Mask Is Not the Object: Volumetric Supervision for 3D Gaussian Segmentation"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21383"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.21383"}
  summary={"3D Gaussian Splatting enables object-level operations on Gaussian primitives, yet existing segmentation methods are largely trained and evaluated through rendered views. This mismatch can produce accurate rendered masks from incomplete 3D selections since alpha-blended feature supervision concentrates learning on dominant contributors while leaving occluded or low-contribution Gaussians weakly supervised. We propose VODA, a plug-and-play supervision objective that exploits local depth structure to group per-pixel contributors and directly supervises all Gaussians in selected depth clusters, bypassing contribution-weighted gradient delivery. To evaluate volumetric completeness, we introduce Removal-3D, a synthetic object-removal benchmark, and the Removal Depth Error (RDE), a metric comparing predicted and ground-truth depth changes. Across four rendering-based baselines, VODA consistently improves volumetric segmentation on Removal-3D while maintaining or improving performance on LERF and ScanNet. Further analysis shows that VODA recovers lower-contribution Gaussians, while simple gradient reweighting and spatial propagation fail to reproduce comparable gains."}

<PaperCard
  title={"WeVisForensics: Self-Evolving Agent for Omni AI-Generated Video Detection"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21373"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.21373"}
  summary={"Recent advances in video generation call for reliable detection grounded in explicit forensic evidence. However, current methods primarily depend on single-pass chain-of-thought reasoning that overlooks brief or localized artifacts, or on predefined multi-agent workflows that offer limited flexibility in selecting relevant inspections. Even with adaptive investigation, optimizing the investigation policy alone does not directly improve the forensic capabilities of fixed experts. We introduce WeVisForensics, an agentic framework in which a main investigator agent coordinates a self-evolving library of forensic inspector sub-agents spanning visual and audio cues. The investigator learns which artifact type to examine, over which window, and when to conclude, and these interactions provide shared feedback for both levels of agents. Our dual-timescale policy optimization strategy, DTPO, closes this loop through fast reinforcement learning of the investigator and slower refinement of the inspectors, guided by annotated artifact intervals, to report subtle artifacts they once dismissed. Furthermore, we curate ForenCue-30K, combining role-specific forensic annotations and interaction trajectories to supervise individual inspections and their coordination, with an accompanying reinforcement-learning pool. Experiments show that WeVisForensics averages 95.2% detection accuracy across in-domain, out-of-domain and agentic benchmarks, 9.0% above the strongest prior detector, while grounding its verdicts in localized evidence."}

<PaperCard
  title={"Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26444"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.26444"}
  summary={"Embodied intelligence aims to let robots perform complex physical tasks with human-like adaptability and reliability, which requires three complementary capabilities: history-based cognitive reasoning, precise and timely physical execution, and the composition of reasoning and execution through environmental feedback. Yet existing benchmarks either evaluate reasoning and execution within the same tasks or assess them separately without their composition, so they reveal little about whether a failure comes from reasoning, execution, or their composition. We introduce Mind2Act World, a diagnostic benchmark of 30 simulated manipulation tasks from five real-world application domains. Each task has three difficulty levels and two scene settings (Clean and Random), yielding 180 configurations. The tasks form three views: Mind tests decisions based on past observations, Act tests precise and timely execution, and Mind2Act combines both in closed-loop tasks where action outcomes guide later decisions. Comparing the three views shows whether a system lacks a capability or fails to compose capabilities it already has. A diagnosis harness further attributes the failed episodes of three representative systems to Mind, Act, or their composition and summarizes each system in a profile card. Our evaluation of multimodal agents, vision-language-action models (VLAs), and world-action models (WAMs) shows that competence in a single view does not carry over to Mind2Act tasks. Even the strongest agent succeeds in 52.7% of Mind and 39.0% of Act episodes but in only 12.0% of Mind2Act episodes. The strongest agent retains task state but struggles with sustained contact, whereas end-to-end policies execute practiced motions but retain little task state. These findings point to composing reasoning and execution through environmental feedback as the main open problem."}

<PaperCard
  title={"ZeroVLN: Learning to Navigate without Supervised Fine-Tuning of the VLM Brain"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26414"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.26414"}
  summary={"Representative vision-language navigation (VLN) methods typically couple a vision-language model (VLM) brain with a learnable action head and fine-tune the VLM brain on navigation-domain data to predict geometric goals. However, such adaptation can substantially alter base VLM weights and degrade general visual capabilities. We introduce ZeroVLN, a slow-fast navigation framework that achieves effective navigation with zero navigation-domain SFT of the VLM brain. Rather than teaching the brain to predict geometric goals, we equip it with a semantic tool-calling interface for conveying movement decisions to an action expert in natural language. We first adapt the action expert to carry out these movement requests, then jointly improve the brain's movement decisions and the action expert's continuous execution through closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL). Trained on the same navigation episodes, ZeroVLN substantially outperforms representative SFT-based hierarchical VLN methods on standard VLN-CE (continuous environments) benchmarks, while inducing only minor changes to the brain's weights and largely preserving its general capabilities. To our knowledge, ZeroVLN is the first slow-fast VLN framework with zero navigation-domain SFT of the VLM brain, and the first to jointly optimize its slow and fast policies through closed-loop agentic RL in VLN-CE."}

<PaperCard
  title={"Posterior Matching Distillation"}
  arxivUrl={"https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26629"}
  hjfyUrl={"https://hjfy.top/arxiv/2611.26629"}
  summary={"Diffusion distillation accelerates image and video generation by compressing a pretrained teacher's iterative sampling process into a few network evaluations. Distribution-based methods achieve this by matching the student's output distribution to the teacher's. This matching is often driven by reverse-KL objectives, whose mode-seeking bias favors dominant teacher modes at the expense of diversity across samples. We introduce Posterior Matching Distillation (PMD), a new member of the distribution-based distillation family that combines mode-covering posterior sampling with any-step generation. At zero population loss, PMD recovers the teacher's full posterior of clean images at each trained noise level. Its stochastic student supports any-step sampling budgets within the trained timestep grid without further training. To learn these posteriors, PMD matches exponentially reweighted averages of student samples conditioned on a fixed noisy state to corresponding teacher posterior means. Under Gaussian corruption, exponentially tilted posterior mean is a single teacher evaluation at a shifted input. We prove that, matching tilted means over an open neighborhood uniquely identifies the full posterior. PMD thus supervises the entire conditional distribution through a family of mean constraints, without any auxiliary student-score network. Experiments on SDXL and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B demonstrate that PMD achieves the best FID among 4-step methods on both models, while maintaining competitive or leading performance across prompt-alignment and perceptual-quality metrics."}
/>
